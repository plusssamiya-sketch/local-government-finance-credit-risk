# Research Memo

## Title

Fiscal Capacity, Land-Market Revenue, and Local-Government Credit Risk

## Motivation

Local-government financing conditions are shaped by more than bond-market prices alone. During fixed-income research work, credit spreads and yields had to be interpreted alongside fiscal revenue, land-transfer revenue, regional growth, refinancing pressure, maturity structure, and liquidity. This project organizes those variables into a reproducible analytical workflow.

## Method

The workflow starts from a region-month dataset. It calculates credit spread as the difference between weighted yield and benchmark yield, then summarizes financing pressure through issuance, repayment, and net financing. It also tracks the relationship between fiscal indicators and market pricing variables.

## Key Analytical Views

1. Financing pressure: issuance, repayment, and net financing by region.
2. Fiscal support: fiscal revenue and land-transfer revenue trends.
3. Market pricing: weighted yield and credit spread by rating segment.
4. Liquidity: turnover and maturity structure.

## Interpretation

A wider credit spread may reflect weaker fiscal support, lower land-market revenue, shorter refinancing windows, weaker liquidity, rating-segment pressure, or broader market repricing. The portfolio goal is not to prove causality. It is to show how fixed-income research transforms fragmented market and fiscal indicators into structured economic interpretation.

## Limits

The sample data are synthetic/anonymized. A publishable version would require careful public-data sourcing, transparent units, consistent benchmark yields, and clear separation between descriptive analysis and causal claims.
